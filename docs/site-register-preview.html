What makes it advanced, in plain words

  • Ball in court. One owner, shown with how long it has sat there. Handing over is one click and is logged.
  • Due date and escalation. Overdue goes red; past the escalation days it appears on the Atm Head’s desk on its own.
  • Five kinds, one register. Site issue · Query (RFI) · Instruction · Decision · Non-conformance. Same screen, filtered.
  • Cost impact is a field, not a sentence. Flag it and the thread offers “raise a working sheet” — the money never leaves the paper trail.
  • Routing from Stakeholders. A structural query addresses the structural consultant because that is who the project says it is.
  • Closed by the person who raised it, never by the person answering. That is the industry rule and it is what makes the count trustworthy.
  • Nothing is deleted. Edits show as edited, status changes are stamped, and the whole trail prints.

The gaps this removes for everyday users

  • Raising is photo-first on a phone: camera → one line → who. No reference number to invent, no form to learn.
  • Every screen opens on “In my court”, so a person sees their own list before the project’s.
  • Recipients are pre-filled. Getting it to the wrong person needs effort.
  • A thread can never be “open, owned by nobody” — the form will not save without an owner.
  • Voice note and WhatsApp-style photo attach; consultants who never log in can answer by e-mail and the reply lands in the thread.

One list of everyone on the project, cut by discipline. Consultants stop being a separate tab and become a filter here — and because each consultant is pinned to their IN4 party, their work-order value, paid and balance ride along, which is all the old Consultants tab was for.

Why the tree, and not a list

  • It is the budget’s own category → sub-category shape, so a decision sits exactly where the money for it sits. Open a sub-category in Budget and the decision is right there.
  • Ticking a parent ticks its children; a half-ticked parent shows a dash, so 91 rows are set in about a minute.
  • A decision that is still open and has an indent coming up inside its lead time turns red. That is the one number that saves a delay.
  • Superseding is honest: the old spec stays visible with its date, so “we changed the tile in July” has a record.

Three pages, one spine

They are not three modules. Stakeholders says who exists and what each one is responsible for; Decisions says what has to be settled and by when; Discussions is how anything gets from one state to the other. Build them apart and each one quietly needs a list of people typed again.

Stakeholders feeds

  • · who a query of each discipline goes to
  • · who may take a decision in that discipline
  • · the distribution list on every thread
  • · the consultant cost figures, from IN4

Decisions feeds

  • · the red flag on an indent whose spec is not settled
  • · the spec printed on a work order’s BOQ
  • · “decided on” against the budget sub-category

Discussions feeds

  • · a decision, when a thread ends in one
  • · a working sheet, when a thread has cost
  • · the daily digest and the Telegram card
  • · each person’s response time

What CT Hub already has that this reuses

· Categories and sub-categories — 91 on SRAH, already per-project

· @mentions and the mention renderer, from sheet comments

· Notifications, digests, Telegram cards and the switches

· Approvals matrix, for who may close or decide

· IN4 parties, work orders and paid figures

· Project floors, for the location field

· Attachments, the same store the sheets use

· Permissions per module, per role, per person

What it needs that does not exist yet

Seven new tables, all additive — no existing table changes shape. disciplines · project_disciplines · project_stakeholders · threads · thread_posts · thread_events · decisions (with revisions). The existing sheet comments stay where they are and appear in the register as a sixth, read-only kind, so no history is lost and nothing has to be migrated on day one.

Order I would build it in

  1. Stakeholders + disciplines + copy-to-projects. Everything else routes through it, and on its own it already answers “who is the architect on P2”.
  2. Discussions. The register, the drawer, ball-in-court, due dates, the phone capture. Notifications on the existing rails.
  3. Decisions & Specs. The tree, applicability, revisions — then the link back into indents, which is where it earns its keep.

Each step is usable on its own and none of them touches Approvals.

Decisions I need from you before building

  1. The word “discipline” is already taken. In CT Hub today a discipline means a budget category (Civil, Electrical, Interiors). The stakeholder sense is different (Architecture, Structural, MEP). I would call the new one Discipline on screen and rename the budget one to Category, which is what everyone calls it anyway — or keep both and live with the collision.
  2. Can consultants and contractors log in? Full answer-in-the-thread access, or replies by e-mail only. It changes who sees project figures.
  3. Who may close a thread — only the raiser, or also the Atm Head.
  4. Escalation days — after how many days does an unanswered thread land on the Atm Head, and does it go further to the Project Head.
  5. Does a cost-impact thread create a working sheet automatically, or only offer the button.
